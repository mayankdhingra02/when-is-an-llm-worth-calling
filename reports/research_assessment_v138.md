# Research assessment after V137–V138

There is a concrete new controlled result, but not a defensible certification that the original benefit-aware escalation claim is ready for a Q2 journal. Journal rank is not an experimental stopping criterion. The next research decision should follow evidence quality and a fixed scope, not whether another batch turns positive.

V138 prospectively collected two cheap neighborhood controls on all six exposed systems and all five original seeds: 60 B20 arms, 600 newly charged recorded outcomes, zero new model calls. Adaptive incumbent-neighbor search beats the historical valid one-shot Qwen3-8B result in 15 cases, ties 14 and loses 1. Fixed-prefix neighbor is 14/14/2. These are descriptive counts across 30 system/seed cases, not 30 independent systems. All six families and all individual results are retained.

Against continued sequential3NN, adaptive is 9/14/7 and fixed is 8/14/8. Both neighborhood controls have positive mean gains on BerkeleyDB and LLVM, negative means on DUNE, HIPAcc, OpenVPN and SAC. DUNE's −11.1482% mean relative contrast shows why win counts alone are insufficient. Neither control uniformly supersedes the classical optimizer.

On the optional two-system V136 feedback comparison, both cheap controls win three LLVM cases and tie two, with no SAC differences across five seeds. Adaptive mean gain is +1.0713% on LLVM and zero on SAC. The denominator is two systems, ten cases; do not substitute it for the full six-system comparison. Historical response costs and original SAC failures remain preserved. The repaired valid SAC treatment is identified explicitly.

Together with V136, this supports a narrow methodological observation: in these recorded domains, improvement over a B10 prefix can be obtained by inexpensive local search, and changed feedback responses do not necessarily improve the final incumbent. Benchmarking LLM escalation needs cheap local-search and continued-search controls at the same objective budget. V138 does not prove those heuristics caused the LLM outputs, establish a universal negative result for LLMs, or reproduce SNAP2/LLAMBO exactly.

The present candidate paper is therefore better framed as an exploratory empirical study of when apparent escalation gains fail stronger controls, with reliability, budget and provenance evidence. A successful predictive escalation controller is not established. Earlier positive exceptions, failed attempts and native results must remain in the synthesis. Do not retrofit a success claim or combine heterogeneous budgets/metrics as though all experiments were exchangeable.

The main unresolved validity gap remains an independent, coherent task cohort. V137 tested an additional owner's data against pre-outcome coverage, metric, lineage and fixed-utility criteria; it did not admit a new cohort. Node.js needs a traceable numbered-table/workload map; GCC/ImageMagick's conservative fixed-utility partitions are too small; Poppler/XZ miss coverage; Lingeling's proxy metric is not solve time. More exposed seeds, prompts, or model calls cannot fix that gap.

Priorities: (1) resolve an outcome-blind independent cohort and fixed task utility, then freeze practical effect/cost margins and group-level analysis before acquiring targets; (2) carry forward sequential3NN, fixed/adaptive neighbor and the fixed feedback/masked model treatments without retuning on that cohort; (3) validate representative native correctness/noise and replicate on a second model/host where feasible. If sufficient independent groups cannot be assembled, scope the claim honestly as exploratory evidence and infrastructure rather than learned-router generalization. Current local model/hardware works; no resource approval is pending.
