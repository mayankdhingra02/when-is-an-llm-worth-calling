# Research assessment after V144–V145

The Spark extension establishes **some useful escalation opportunities, but no successful benefit-aware controller**. Across five fixed Spark workloads and five seeds, SmolLM3-3B exceeded both sequential3NN and adaptive-neighbor continuations by more than the frozen1% margin in four cases, spread across Wordcount, PageRank, Terasort and TPC-H. Qwen3-8B did so in one Terasort case. These are real local-model responses and charged recorded outcomes, not synthetic examples or selected reruns.

Always calling still lost on average. SmolLM gain was −0.1099% versus sequential and −0.4168% versus adaptive; Qwen −1.9009% and −2.2181%. SmolLM's near-zero sequential mean does not establish equivalence. Its seven >1% sequential wins were balanced by seven >1% harms. Qwen repeated192/250 proposed prototypes; SmolLM23/240, although deterministic projection still produced ten unique acquisitions per arm. This is a recorded diagnostic of proposal diversity, not proof of its causal effect.

Both frozen controllers chose zero calls. They missed seven practical SmolLM wins versus sequential, including four wins against both strong controls. Every Spark case exceeded the controller development range for mean feature-domain cardinality. Therefore, neither avoiding average harm nor choosing no calls proves successful benefit prediction. The hindsight upper reference was1.1843% mean gain for SmolLM versus sequential, not an implementable policy. This supplies more informative routing variation than V141's zero joint practical wins on six exposed nominal-data families.

The main model/sequential/adaptive comparisons are complete for all25cases/model. One TPC-H source duration was missing in a random-row arm, making that comparison inconclusive. Strict validation stopped V144; an orchestration error nevertheless launched six SmolLM requests before shutdown. Five completed and one was interrupted. V145 froze an explicit missing-data amendment, preserved the originals and consumed only the unused allowance. It used classical fallback for the interrupted intent without retry. The batch is therefore an exploratory recovery, not pristine confirmation. The raw missing cell never became an invented runtime.

This adds **one software family**, not five independent systems. Source authors ran Spark2.2.1 on an AWS cluster; this study queried their released tables locally. It does not replicate native Spark performance/noise/correctness on this Mac. Public benchmark contamination, source failure-denominator gaps and dataset redistribution rights remain unresolved. Numeric proposal representation changed prospectively from prior nominal experiments; all workloads/seeds stay grouped. The source audit, private source files, exact pins, raw prompts/responses, acquisition logs, synthetic tests, independent replay and deterministic figures are available.

The evidence now supports a focused research question: **can a controller recognize rare, model-specific improvements in numeric configuration spaces without paying for harmful escalations?** It does not yet support a generalizable routing method, equivalence claim, independent-family confidence interval, or a journal-quartile readiness guarantee. The next useful evidence is a frozen confirmation on additional independent numeric-configuration families, with a development-only controller and explicit missing/failure rules. More tuning on these Spark outcomes would be development work and must be labeled as such.
