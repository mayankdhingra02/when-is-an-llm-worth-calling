# V156: source, environment and exposure audit

Two new engine candidates were retrieved from the official Python package registry: cvc5 1.4.1 and Google OR-Tools 9.14.6206. Exact CPython3.10/macOSarm64 wheels, registry SHA256, owner links and all twelve dependency pins are saved in artifacts/sources/v156 and configs/solvers_v156.lock.txt. The original experiment environment remains unchanged; .venv-solvers156 is separate and installation used only local wheels with --no-index --require-hashes. pip check passed. Installed engine versions were queried without solving; 187 native library hashes are saved in artifacts/study_v156/audit.json. Installing/importing does not establish correctness or performance.

Owner verification: [cvc5 release](https://github.com/cvc5/cvc5/releases/tag/cvc5-1.4.1) identifies tag cvc5-1.4.1 and commit2b2e844; [OR-Tools release](https://github.com/google/or-tools/releases/tag/v9.14) identifies v9.14 and commit0d60e8a. These are upstream identities, not locally built source commits; the executable provenance is the exact registry wheel hash. cvc5's wheel describes itself as BSD version; its COPYING includes modified-BSD terms and caveats about optional GPL library builds. Bundled license notices were preserved rather than inferring every component's license from that label. OR-Tools wheel carries Apache2.0. Full packaged license/copyright notices for all dependencies are copied under artifacts/study_v156/licenses; no redistribution/publication occurred.

[Google's N-queens example](https://developers.google.com/optimization/cp/queens) supplies the task semantics: one queen per column, no shared rows or diagonals. The project implements its own small binding/validator using those mathematical constraints. The forthcoming native probes are generated benchmark inputs, with actual solver-produced solutions and timings; they are neither recorded industrial datasets nor fabricated LLM outputs. Both engines share a constraint-solving domain and a standard, publicly known task. Z3 has earlier project exposure, so do not claim complete domain novelty or two independent industrial workloads.
