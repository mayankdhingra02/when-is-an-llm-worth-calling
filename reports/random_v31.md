# V31: random controls reveal almost no shortlist headroom beyond the stronger baseline

**In nine of ten saved Opus/Z3 cases, adaptive shortlist centroid already attained the best target available in the prefix plus twenty-row shortlist.** Any method restricted to that candidate set, including an LLM, has zero possible improvement over this comparator in those cases. The remaining case (Z3, seed 11) permits at most a 0.131810% target reduction, from 4.552 to 4.546. This is an evaluator-only hindsight ceiling, not a measured LLM result or an online routing rule. It applies to these saved prefixes, this candidate pool and this recorded objective.

Twenty new random continuations actually ran, using 200 charged objective acquisitions. Exact conditional random expectations were calculated separately and checked by independent combinatorial counts and exhaustive enumeration. The result strengthens the case for retaining the adaptive shortlist comparator and for avoiding further LLM experiments on these same candidate pools.

## Frozen follow-up and actual execution

This is an exploratory follow-up after inspecting V30, not fresh held-out evidence. The [protocol](protocol_v31_random.md) and [156 input references](protocol_v31_random.freeze.json) were frozen before the random-arm acquisitions. Both families were already exposed; no new group was admitted. No parameter or seed search occurred.

For each of the ten V30 cases, reuse the exact saved ten-label prefix and acquired-only twenty-row shortlist. Run two continuations: sample ten rows uniformly without replacement from all unacquired candidates, and sample ten from the shortlist. Separate SHA256-derived RNG seeds use the dataset, case seed and fixed mode string. Selection sees candidate IDs and prefix state only; the full objective table is read by the evaluator after collection. Each arm retains the prefix incumbent, clones state and acquires ten distinct new labels, finishing with twenty evaluations.

All **20/20 arms completed**, with no failed runs, retries or omitted cases. There were **zero model calls**. The two methods were fixed before execution and were not revised after these outcomes. Raw events are in [acquisitions.jsonl](../results/v31_random/acquisitions.jsonl), with per-arm states, seeds, selections and checkpoints in the same directory.

## What the ceiling means

The exact random distribution gives zero probability of strict improvement over adaptive shortlist centroid in all five Opus cases and four Z3 cases. Independent source scoring confirms the comparator target equals the minimum over the prefix and shortlist in each of those nine cases. In Z3/11, a random ten-row subset has a 50% chance of including a better row, but the maximum target reduction is only 0.131810%. The equal-family hindsight ceiling across all ten cases is 0.013181%, before any inference cost or reliability penalty. It is not a learned policy result or a claim of practical equivalence.

This interpretation was recorded after the frozen probability analysis; it is explicitly post hoc. It does not exclude improvements from candidates outside the shortlist, different initial prefixes, other budgets or other software systems. The full-space random distributions can have strict-improvement probability even when the shortlist cannot. Nor does it prove an LLM will fail elsewhere. It establishes limited opportunity for the specific proposed escalation choice once a stronger classical comparator is included.

## Limits and next action

Two exposed families and five seeds each remain insufficient for broad generalization. No new router or LLM evaluation occurred. Opus bitrate/channel/sample-rate changes do not establish equal-audio-quality utility; absolute target units and per-row measurement uncertainty remain unresolved. Candidate restriction and the strong comparator define the narrow ceiling. Repeated exploratory analyses cannot substitute for prospective independent evaluation.

**Next action:** define a quality-constrained task and test for usable headroom on development data before allocating more model calls, then freeze strong comparators and reserve untouched software families for evaluation. Do not screen future test families by observed gains. Retain these negative opportunity results, and do not spend the next inference allowance repeating the same shortlist experiment. Fresh real inference needs a new bounded allowance or compatible provenance-checked cache; none is silently authorized here.
