# Discussion note: when is an LLM worth calling?

**The implemented pilot has not demonstrated useful LLM escalation or a benefit-router advantage.** It does provide reproducible evidence explaining why an apparent improvement can be misleading, and why a task needs measurable room for improvement before routing is worth studying. This note is for review; it has not been sent or submitted anywhere.

The original question remains: after10 cheap configuration evaluations, can predecision information predict whether an LLM should spend the remaining10? SNAP2's discussion already raises conditional escalation; that idea alone is not a novelty claim. The exact SNAP2 artifact was not located in the bounded audit. Our changed-model and classical implementations are adaptations, not its numerical replication. See the [source audit](source_audit.md).

Three observations are supported:

1. **A real model's apparent selection gain can be explained by candidate order.** In15 development cases, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct selected IDs0–9 every time. A first-ten rule reproduces all choices without inference. The subsequent exhaustive subset diagnostic found that uniform random selection matches or beats each observed LLM score with probability at least0.5. This is a conditional reference, not a significance test or a general statement about stronger models. [V8](pilot_report_v8.md), [V9](concrete_result.md).
2. **The held-out pilot does not establish routing benefit.** On three held-out software families/five seeds each, the development-fitted benefit and uncertainty policies selected no escalation. Always escalating was worse on average than staying classical. Three test families are insufficient for broad learned-router claims; seeds do not increase the number of independent systems. [V6](pilot_report_v6.md).
3. **A larger candidate grid did not create substantial opportunity on the local development workload.** We actually measured846 new compression trials over282 settings, verified every roundtrip, then ran30 paired classical arms. Mean remaining recorded feasible-runtime headroom after cheap search was0.537%/0.197%/0% on Zstandard/LZ4/zlib. Small effects and timing noise preclude superiority/equivalence claims. This is one small archive and exposed families, not a realistic application-wide evaluation. [V17](expanded_grid_v17.md).

The repository includes actual prompts/responses, failed-run denominators, pinned inputs, physical payloads, charged objectives, separate collection/deployment accounting, frozen protocols, independent decision replay and120 passing tests. Source records and binaries/data remain subject to their licenses; this is a local review repository, not a remotely published package.

A defensible discussion is whether the combination of candidate-order controls and a pre-inference headroom check merits a small negative/methodological result. Novelty, publication and a positive LLM effect remain unestablished. The next action is to agree on an application-grounded runtime/size task before further model collection. Any follow-up should use representative workloads, stronger cheap controls, reliable timing and genuinely untouched evaluation groups, with a fixed stopping rule. The present128-call follow-up allowance is exhausted; no new campaign has been started.
