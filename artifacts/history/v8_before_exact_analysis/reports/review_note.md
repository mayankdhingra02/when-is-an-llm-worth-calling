# Research discussion note — local pilot, not a paper claim

**Current finding:** this small-model pilot has not established that benefit-aware LLM escalation improves software-configuration optimization. It has established a reproducible paired evaluation and identified failure mechanisms worth separating before scaling a router. Negative results here concern this model, formulation, tables and budgets; they do not refute LLM optimization generally.

The repository asks whether, after 10 acquired outcomes, a controller can decide whether to spend its remaining 10 evaluations on an LLM. The classical algorithm is an explicitly labeled paper-based nominal-centroid adaptation. SNAP2's exact artifact was not established; this is not a numerical replication. Primary-source and license checks are recorded in source_audit.md, admission_v6.md and THIRD_PARTY.md. The dataset and model versions, feature/target schema, raw prompts, acquired labels, costs and frozen protocols are saved.

The strongest existing routing evidence is v6: 30 paired cases across six software families, split into three development and three test groups, with five fixed seeds per group. A development-fit gain predictor and its threshold were sealed before test acquisitions. On the 15 held-out cases, the real local 0.5B model materially helped twice and harmed three times at a fixed .02 normalized-loss margin. Always escalation had mean loss .074581 against .061505 for never escalation. The benefit and uncertainty controllers both chose zero escalations, so this evaluation does not show that benefit prediction can select useful calls. A matched-zero-rate random comparison is necessarily uninformative about ranking. Hindsight loss .055799 is a nondeployable upper reference, not an achieved routing result.

That result motivated a mechanism diagnosis. In v6, 279/300 generated feature proposals copied observed prefix configurations, and 289/300 required projection onto available unevaluated table rows. In the subsequent development-only v7 experiment, a decoder constraint eliminated original-prefix copies while keeping prompt and other treatment components fixed. All 15 real model continuations completed; none materially improved and one materially worsened against each main comparator. Only 7/150 new proposals matched recorded candidates directly. Missing table rows are not necessarily invalid software configurations. Syntax and prefix exclusion therefore did not establish useful exploration.

V8 tested direct selection from an acquired-label-only shortlist of 20 existing rows. All 30 matched controls and 15 real local-model continuations completed, with 450 new target acquisitions. Mean normalized loss improved by .005349 versus adaptive classical continuation (2/15 material benefits, zero material harms), but the raw responses expose a decisive limitation: **the model selected IDs 0–9, the first ten displayed candidates, in every case**. Candidate order was randomly assigned, so the recorded choices are exactly reproduced by taking the first half of that shuffled list. This observed rule has the design distribution of a random subset. The better realized scores therefore do not demonstrate task-sensitive ranking or incremental model value. This is a post-hoc diagnostic of saved evidence, not an additional collected control. It makes no claim about behavior under untested prompts or reordered lists.

The complete v8 evidence passed 64 tests and independent replay of all 45 branch states and 150 model choices. Its 15 real requests used 23,930 input tokens, 300 output tokens and 28.661 seconds request wall time; startup cost was 3.800 seconds separately. External spending stayed USD 0. Raw prompts, tokens, masks and outcomes remain intact, and the approved request cap is now exhausted at 128/128. The concise result is a reliability warning backed by execution: valid novel candidate IDs and improved aggregate quality can still conceal a model-free selection rule.

The evidence is sufficient for a small, candid methods discussion: real execution, explicit failures, fair per-arm acquisition budgets, grouped splits and a falsifiable next comparison. It is insufficient for cross-system generalization or publication-level claims about a learned router. Three test groups, a tiny single model, binary features, one target, one budget and exposed public data are material limitations. Development ablations must not be pooled as fresh test evidence. Results were not selected for being positive.

Questions to resolve at review:

1. Is there enough attainable LLM benefit to justify learning a controller, or should a stronger continuation mechanism be established first?
2. Is direct candidate selection the right offline task, and what stronger cheap baseline should define the opportunity cost of escalation?
3. Which new, independently validated software families and practical improvement margin would make a held-out test meaningful?

The frozen V8 comparison is now complete. Stop adaptive treatment search for this pilot and review all results. The next research priority is a predeclared candidate-order permutation test against a first-half selection rule: establish whether any candidate continuation responds to configuration evidence before scaling a learned router. A stronger local model could be a separately fixed treatment. Broad evaluation requires a separately specified untouched-system design and new resources. No email, submission or publication has been made.
